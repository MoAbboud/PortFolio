prototype

Portable memory for AI chat. Paste a conversation with Claude or ChatGPT and this keeps the part worth keeping: the decisions, constraints, preferences and open threads it established, compact enough to paste into the next chat.

Everything here runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, no API key, no model download.

The brief

Paste this into a new chat. Ordered by what matters most, cut to fit the budget.

What did not fit

Kept, and traceable, just not in this render. Raise the budget and they come back.

What this prototype is, and what it leaves out

The full pipeline is capture, extract, merge, render, serve, verify. Three of those are pure rules over text and run here exactly as they do on the server - the patterns on this page are generated from the same Python source, and a test fails if they drift.

Runs here, unchanged

  • Extract - only what you said is read. A chat is mostly the assistant talking, and treating its suggestions as fact fills the memory with ideas you turned down.
  • Render - constraints first, then decisions, then open threads. A model that knows the rules can behave correctly without every fact; one with the facts and not the rules cannot.
  • Residue - sentences no entry captured, spending only the budget left over.

Left on the server

  • Merge needs sentence embeddings and a DeBERTa cross-encoder - gigabytes of weights. It decides whether a new claim repeats a stored one, refines it, or retires it.
  • So this page can spot that you changed your mind - same rule the real merge step uses - but not resolve which entry the change replaces. That is an entailment judgement.
  • Token counts here are estimates; the real pipeline counts with cl100k.

Measured on eight long conversations against 261 hand-written facts, the full pipeline recalls 0.88 of them at a 3,000-token budget while carrying none of the 40 claims that had been reversed or refused. A plain "summarise this" at the same budget manages 0.21, and pasting the most recent text 0.31. The numbers, the dead ends and what the judge got wrong are in the repository.
